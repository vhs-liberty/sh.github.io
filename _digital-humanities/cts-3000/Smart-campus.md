## Applying the ideas of the ["smart city"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_city) to building a smart University of Guelph campus

*Introduction*

For the *GuelphU Open Data Portal*, I hereby propose the inclusion of data on reconstructing the undergraduate student body levels of involvement on campus following the lack thereof across certain college governments and the Central Student Association. The report, itself, will be an accumulation of survey data and historical context data. Herein, the data will be presented as separate files, for the purposes of transparency and to allow for further data work, in respect to the platform’s mission.The survey data, which will be expanded upon below, involves a rigorous, month-long campaign, to gathering insight from current students on their feelings towards these organizations and if they are interested or not and why so. As well, the historicity that will be collected will involve mapping the number of candidates that each organization has had in total based on availability of such data, which may show whether that has been declining, steady, or otherwise. This will involve the collecting of data from a variety of archives, interviewing involved individuals for their opinions, and collecting everything into a database, whose metadata you can find below.



In compiling this information, it should be noted that the Graduate Students Association is excluded as the focus is on the Central Student Association, in reason of the issues in the undergraduate student body’s involvement, and certain college governments, where there is no intended differentiation in terms of graduate or undergraduate as that will be noted as an exception to the undergraduate focus to make data collection easier.

*Metadata*Hereby, the database shall be split into the categories of Survey, Timeline, and Written Context. Survey involves data gathered from the responses, the Timeline involves the data of historic involvement, based on recorded past participations, and Written Context involves but is not limited to the digitizing and sharing of past posters, flyers, news, etc. all material which is made of written word that generates historicity. Thereby, each of these categories will be associated with a data type, based on contents; those with text will be string, those with numbers will be integer, and wherein both apply, they will be integer and string. These will be coupled with the example values Framework, Time Period, and Contributing Body(ies). Herein, the Framework is a series of defining parent tags of what each category contains; Time Period defines of what time the data is from; and Contributing Body(ies) is an assortment of individuals, archives, and/or groups which aided in the collection process either directly or indirectly by being a source of information. Note, the values herein defined are examples and therefore do not reflect the final product.

As previously mentioned, there will be the exclusion of the Graduate Students Association, again due to reason of focus on the undergraduate student body. As well, the College Governments act as a parent Contributing Body(ies), wherein these values become more definitive within the database, and are vague due to their potential volatility.

*Collection Process*

The survey, briefly touched on above, will feature four core questions: On a scale of 1 to 5, How Do You Feel Towards the Central Student Association; On a Scale of 1 to 5, How Do You Feel Towards your College Government; What Word Describes Your Interest in Participating; and What Word Describes Your Feeling?

For the “what” questions, the respondents will be able to choose their word, as it is not right to limit them to a drop-down menu. These words may help create a word map, which can be used to better understand the causes and potential solutions to this issue. This survey will be distributed in collaboration with the CSA and advertising campaigns, including, but not limited to the UC Courtyard Banners, Tabling, and the UC Television Screens, to name a few.

The timeline, then, will involve collecting data from interviews and digitalizing important changes to structure. This involves interview transcripts, text only, wherein with the goal is gathering historical insight from involved individuals. No audio will be collected due to privacy concerns. Then there is the digitalizing and archiving of non-historicity posters, news, flyers, ads, etc. all of which will be collected by communicating with the respective heads.

*Storage & Sharing*

Of all that is collected will be stored on an encrypted hard disk drive (HDD) and backed up on an optical Blu-Ray disc, to be stored in an undisclosed location known only to the researchers. This will be the temporary place of storage during the acquisition process, as afterwards it will be stored on the GuelphU Open Data Portal servers, wherein the HDD and disc will be wiped clean. It is aimed that all parties, be it students, faculty, staff, and/or community members, have access to this information, with any personal data permanently deleted during the acquisition and permanent storage phases. Personal data is hereby defined as any that involves the information of any individual or group which could put them in harm’s way.

In doing so, it is acknowledged that there is one major benefit and one major downfall to this project. The benefit is that, with such information, these organizations will become able to initiate programs targeted at resolving the lack of student engagement and the possibility for “offshoot” data projects, which may use this work to produce greater insights. However, herein lies the con, that parties with wrongful intentions may use this to catalyze the problem, for example a group may employ this data for the purposes of maintaining a lack of engagement, so the executives of said agency naturally end up maintaining a sort of “power monopoly”. Level of harm to students herein is contested since one reason for lack of engagement may be due to a notion of pre-existing corruption, wherein such a “power monopoly” already exists. If it were to persist, or potentially be discovered as true, it may lead to internal policy changes with the University Executives.

*Ethics & Concluding Opinion*

Due to the sensitivity of such a project, all collected data will be done under exclusive consent, and efforts will be pursued to ensure that consent is not due to emotional or reward-based afflictions. That is, there shall be no reward offered, nor will any personal information be collected in any of the processes, and in the scenario where it may be needed, such as transcripts, the individual chooses a nickname for themselves. This will ensure that people participate of their own infliction, with full clarity of what their information will be used for before any collecting begins. In terms of defining efforts, these include but are not limited to deleting any and all communications in case of a hack, deleting draft files, and utilizing privacy-focused tools such as Tor or Proton Mail. The only issue here will arise from existing surveillance cameras, for which identity will be masked as necessary, to ensure that all published data contains only the data and is not traceable to any entity by any advanced or beginner means. This also includes any individual who may be okay with having their names public, wherein the importance of privacy may be explained.

Altogether, this is because of the previously mentioned uncertainty of how this data could be used in negative means. The aim is to understand why student involvement decreased, and in doing so, aid in bringing it up; however, there is always the possibility of its use to maintain the low involvement. This will be countered, as necessary, with further research, either through refining the data set and its report, the key part of this proposal, or by other means as applicable.
